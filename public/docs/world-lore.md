# Chronicle of the Clans

*A companion reference to "The Long Song." Read this if you want to understand the world before you play - the land, its five clans, and the slow catastrophe pressing in on all of them.*

---

## The World

The world is shrinking.

Not destroyed the way fire destroys, or crushed the way war crushes. It is **unmade**.

A place that existed yesterday might not exist today. Not blown up. Not buried. Not flooded. Simply erased - no ruins, no grave, no ash. Where something once stood, there is only a flat stretch of ground with no colour, no smell, no sound.

Worse than the vanishing itself is what it does to memory. Losses fade or blur. People sense that something is wrong without being able to say what. A family carries a gap in its history. A song is missing a verse. A map shows an edge that no longer matches the world.

Five clans live on what remains. They do not trust each other. They blame each other. Each preserves the past in its own way - and each, without fully realizing it, holds only a piece of the same truth.

The story begins the moment that truth can no longer be kept apart.

---

## The Rules of the Unmaking

The Unmaking is not chaos. It follows rules - which is exactly why it can, perhaps, be reasoned with.

**It takes the thing, and weakens the memory of the thing.** When something is unmade, it usually disappears both physically and from people's conscious memory. But the erasure is rarely total. Three kinds of traces can survive: *structural* traces (foundations, empty spaces, errors in maps or records), *ritual* traces (a pause in a song, a tradition no one understands anymore, an incomplete ledger), and *emotional* traces (grief, unease, or longing with no known cause). People often don't remember *what* is gone - only that *something* is.

**What is held tightly in memory resists.** Things remembered often and by many people stand up better against the Unmaking. Such things are called **anchors** - a song, a stone, a map, a book, a name, or even a deliberately preserved silence. An anchor doesn't make something immortal. It only makes it harder to erase.

**It moves toward old power.** The Unmaking doesn't hunt people. It is drawn to places where the old power, **the Weave**, was once used heavily - old cities, mines, shrines, riverbeds, gathering places.

**It has been here a long time, but it is speeding up.** Three generations have lived with it. People have grown used to it, explained it away, built whole cultures around the loss. But something has changed recently: it is moving faster now. More places are being taken closer together in time. More anchors are showing cracks at once. That is why this story is happening *now*.

**It can be negotiated with.** The Unmaking is not a monster. It is not evil. It is not angry. It is a **collection** - a debt coming due. That means it can be met, understood, and perhaps bargained with - but only by people who understand what the Weave really was, what was borrowed, and what price the world is now asking back.

## The World Before: One People

No one knows this for certain when the story begins. It is the truth the player uncovers in pieces.

Long ago, the five clans were **one people**. They lived closer together, built larger, and used a power they called **the Weave**. With it they could make things last longer, grow bigger, and stretch further than they should. They grew grain in bare stone. They held up buildings on ground too weak to bear them. They guided water where it would never naturally flow.

But they did not own the Weave. They **borrowed** from it.

The price was never paid in gold. It was paid with what already existed in the world: a name, a memory, a landscape, a river, a bloodline, a place. At first the cost was small and scattered - no one saw the whole picture. By the time they understood what was happening, the debt was already too large to pay together.

So they did the only thing they had time for: they split the original agreement into five parts, gave one part to each group, and scattered. They hid knowledge from themselves and from each other, and built enough distance and mistrust that no single group could easily gather it all again.

That doesn't mean today's hostility is fake. Only the *start* of it was engineered. Real conflicts grew afterward - fights over water, lies buried in archives, unfair trade, old wounds and shame, pride and inheritance. The hatred between the clans today is both **inherited and real**.

## The Map

What remains of the world is roughly the size of a small country. Around its edges - and in a growing patch cutting in from one side - lies **the Blank**.

- The **Mountain Clan** lives in the highlands, controlling the springs, the stone, and the mines.
- The **Water Clan** lives in the delta, which has been drying out ever since the river was dammed.
- The **City Clan** lives at the centre, controlling the roads, the bridges, the archives, and the law.
- The **Trader Clan** holds no land of its own, but controls passage, goods, and information.
- The **Forest Clan** lives closest to the Blank, and survives there in a way no other clan could.

## The Five Clans

Every clan is built the same way: a landscape, a resource, a way of remembering, a strength, a weakness, an inner conflict, and an anchor.

> All five remember the world - but each in a completely different way. Each method preserves something vital, and blinds them to something else at the same time.

### The Mountain Clan

**Landscape:** Highlands, mountain halls, deep shafts, cold dry stone chambers.
**Resource:** Ore, stone, and depth.
**Way of remembering:** They carve it into stone. Everything important should be readable in the walls.
**Strength:** Stability, endurance, history. They hold the oldest anchor.
**Weakness:** Slowness, pride, and a culture where admitting a mistake is dangerous.
**Inner conflict:** The young want to reopen the deepest shaft. The elders refuse - and no longer remember exactly why.
**Anchor:** The First Stone.
**Standing conflict with the Water Clan:** Two generations ago, the Mountain Clan dammed the river to survive and run their mines. It dried out the delta downstream.

### The Water Clan

**Landscape:** A delta of boats, docks, ropework, mudflats, and shallow water.
**Resource:** Fish, salt, and passage over water.
**Way of remembering:** They sing it. History, names, losses, and promises live on in verses and refrains.
**Strength:** Mobility, community, living memory.
**Weakness:** If a verse dies out, a whole piece of the past can die with it. Their homeland is drying up.
**Inner conflict:** Stay and fight for the delta, or let go and become a people of the open sea?
**Anchor:** The Long Song.

### The City Clan

**Landscape:** The last great city, built over the ruins of something older.
**Resource:** Law, organization, writing, infrastructure.
**Way of remembering:** They archive it - maps, laws, lists, and documents keep the world in order.
**Strength:** Order, coordination, the ability to compare knowledge.
**Weakness:** A memory can be *falsified* here. When gaps appear, leaders sometimes fill them with comfortable lies to keep the peace.
**Inner conflict:** Younger archivists have discovered the forgeries, and don't know whether the truth will save the city or tear it apart.
**Anchor:** The True Map.

### The Trader Clan

**Landscape:** Roads, crossroads, caravans, waystations. They have hubs, but no real homeland.
**Resource:** Goods, passage, information, deals.
**Way of remembering:** They keep ledgers. Every favor, every debt, every delivery, every negotiation is written down.
**Strength:** They move between every other clan and know exactly who needs what.
**Weakness:** No one fully trusts them. They have profited from the world falling apart, even as they help hold it together.
**Inner conflict:** An old debt threatens to ruin the clan. Some want to pay it by selling information that should never be sold.
**Anchor:** The Ledger of Unpaid Debts.

### The Forest Clan

**Landscape:** Deep old forest, right at the edge of the Blank. No permanent settlements.
**Resource:** Medicine, hunting, hidden paths, and hard-won experience living near unmade ground.
**Way of remembering:** They forget on purpose. They believe holding on too tightly draws the Unmaking closer, so they deliberately let things go.
**Strength:** They understand the pattern of the Unmaking better than anyone, from long, close experience.
**Weakness:** They have let go of so much that they risk losing themselves entirely.
**Inner conflict:** The young want to start remembering again. The elders believe it will bring the Unmaking straight to them.
**Anchor:** The Empty Name.

---

## Conflict on Four Levels

**Inside each clan:** Young against old. Change against tradition. The young want to do something new; the elders forbid it, often without remembering the whole reason why.

**Between two clans:** The blood feud between the Mountain and Water Clans - the dam and the dying delta. Both sides carry real damage and real arguments.

**Between the blocs:** The Mountain and City Clans are held together by necessity. The Water and Trader Clans are held together by mutual use. The Forest Clan stands outside both - and everyone wants what they know.

**Everyone against the Unmaking:** The largest conflict of all - and one that cannot be resolved until something is done about the other three levels first.

---

## The Anchors and the Fragments

The five anchors are five different ways of storing the very same original agreement. They are not five keys to five locks - they are one language, expressed in five forms: stone, song, map, debt, and silence.

No single clan can read all five alone. Cooperation between them isn't just the right thing to do - it's the only thing that actually works.

Over the course of the story, fragments of the truth surface from each clan's side of things. The more of them come together, the more of the full picture becomes visible - and the more choices open up for how the story can end.
